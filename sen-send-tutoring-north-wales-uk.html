A supportive place to learn

SEN and SEND tutoring should adapt to the learner, not force the learner to adapt to the lesson

Some students need more thinking time. Some benefit from shorter explanations, repeated examples, written steps or a more predictable lesson structure. Others may understand a topic well but find exam questions, organisation or confidence much harder.

Our aim is to match each student with a tutor who can respond to those individual differences. Lessons can be adjusted around pace, communication, confidence and the way the student processes new information.

We do not assume that two learners with the same SEN, SEND or ALN label will learn in the same way. We focus on what actually helps the individual student understand, participate and make progress.

What a supportive lesson can look like

  • Clear instructions without unnecessary overload
  • Topics broken into smaller, manageable steps
  • Extra processing and thinking time where useful
  • Repetition without judgement
  • Regular checks for understanding
  • A consistent one-to-one relationship
  • Exam practice introduced at an appropriate pace
SEN, SEND & ALN in Wales

What is the difference between SEN, SEND and ALN?

Families use several different terms when looking for additional educational support. In Wales, the current statutory term is Additional Learning Needs (ALN). SEN and SEND remain familiar terms used by many parents and are also widely used elsewhere in the UK.

Whichever term your family or school uses, our tutoring focuses on the individual learner and the academic support that helps them access their subject more confidently.

Welsh Government information about ALN
SEN

Special Educational Needs.

SEND

Special Educational Needs and Disabilities.

ALN

Additional Learning Needs, the current term used in Wales.

Tutoring supports academic learning and confidence, but it does not diagnose additional needs or replace statutory ALN provision, an IDP, school support or specialist educational and health services.
Personalised tutoring

How online SEN, SEND and ALN tutoring can be adapted

The exact approach depends on the learner and tutor, but one-to-one tutoring provides flexibility that is difficult to achieve when every student must follow the same whole-class pace.

Flexible pacing

More time can be spent on difficult concepts without pressure to keep pace with a whole class.

Smaller steps

Longer problems and unfamiliar topics can be broken into shorter stages with clear checkpoints.

Repetition when useful

A student can revisit a method or explanation without feeling that they should already know it.

Clear written structure

Tutors can use worked examples, written steps and structured notes alongside spoken explanations.

Low-pressure questioning

Questions can check understanding without making the whole lesson feel like a test.

Confidence before speed

Lessons can prioritise secure understanding first, before increasing independence and exam pace.
